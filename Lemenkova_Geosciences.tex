% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\) 
\abstract{This paper explores spatial variability of the ten climatic variables of Mongolia in 2019: average minimal and maximal temperatures, wind speed, soil moisture, downward surface shortwave radiation (DSRAD), snow water equivalent (SWE), vapor pressure deficit, vapor pressure anomaly, monthly precipitation and Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI). The PDSI demonstrates the simplified soil water balance estimating relative soil moisture conditions in Mongolia. The research presents mapping of the climate datasets derived from TerraClimate open source repository of the meteorological and climate measurements in NetCDF format. The methodology presented the compiled observations of Mongolia visualized by machine learning approach using Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) cartographic scripting toolset. The results present 10 new maps of climate data over Mongolia made using automated cartographic techniques of GMT. Spatial environmental and climate analysis, which determines relative distribution of PDSI and temperature extremes, precipitation and soil moisture, wind speed and DSRAD shown maximum at 491 mm, the DSRAD shown minimum at 40.000 Wm-2, maximum at 113.000 Wm-2 in the Gobi Desert region, SWE (up to 491 mm), VAP and VPD compared with landmass parameters represent powerful cartographic tools to address complex regional climate and environmental issues in Mongolia, a country with contrasting topography, extreme climate conditions and unique environmental setting.}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background}
Climate has long been recognized to play an important role in the Earth’s environmental dynamics \cite{Dashkhuu}, functionality \cite{Natsagdorj,An}, variability and sustainability in various regions of the Earth \cite{SATTLER2021101620,Jiang,Lemenkova2021a,DUGERDIL2021107235}, which has been a subject of numerous studies. This especially concerns Mongolia (Fig. \ref{fig1}), a country with extreme continental climate, severe colds winters and dry summers with extremal hot temperatures, presence of permafrost and cryogenic morphostructures \cite{Dashtseren2021,Tsedevdorj2021,Blomdin}. Being a country sensitive to the climate extreme and ecological variations affecting its fragile ecosystems, Mongolia presents an example of unique region of the Earth influenced by global environmental and climate change. Climatic variables, such as variations in temperature, frequency and intensity of precipitation, direction and strength of winds have a strong effect on the environmental setting of the country correlating with major distribution of the eco-zones and topographic relief. 

As a result, climate variability influences affects various aspects of vegetation coverage: agricultural potential and crop production \cite{Otgonbayar,Lemenkova2021b,Munkhtur}, distribution and health conditions of forests and vegetation \cite{Erdenebaatar,WANG2022e02034}. Besides, climate has effects on social factors, such as tourism potential which includes nomadic tourism \cite{OGORMAN2007161,BUCKLEY200847}, distribution of nature attractions, such as national parks and reserves, aesthetics, quality and suitability of the landscapes for visits and recreation \cite{Gantemur,Klauco2017}. Another important point of influence for the climate effects is urbanization which is reflected in the distribution and density of population in Mongolia which naturally depend of the basic human conditions for living \cite{Fan,Khishigdorj,Oyunjargal}. Temperature variability, precipitation and winds are factors essential in the soil moisture and quality \cite{Samdandorj}, vegetation growth and health \cite{Kolar,Lemenkova2020a,MENG2022152198}, and the environmental sustainability \cite{Guo,Lemenkova2020b}. 

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_01.jpg}
	\caption{Topographic map of Mongolia. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app1}. Source: author. \label{fig1}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

At the same time, the two most important climate factors (temperature and precipitation) are largely influenced by the distributions of the relief landforms and local topography of the region wit hnotable differences of the temperature regime, thermal effects of surface characteristics and precipitation patterns in mountains \cite{LEHMKUHL201824,LEHMKUHL201212}, plateau \cite{LUO2021105505,Baoetal2015,CHEN2022108560}, steppes \cite{Zemmrich} and deserts \cite{LI2021105259,LUO201957,RAHMAN2022391,SAAVEDRA2020104711,LEI2021105661}. In turn, key exogenous surface processes such as weathering and erosion, especially active in desert areas and regions with strong winds and hydrological network largely control regional topography by sculpturing and modifying new landforms both in Mongolia and in the neighbouring regions of China \cite{DAI2022115518,XU2020100496,LYU2021111488}. 

Grazing intensity and active cattle heading present additional factors contributing to the environmental pressure and potential degradation of rangelands in the mountain steppes of Mongolia \cite{Otgontuya,Khishigdorj,ADDISON201337}. Modified ecosystems affect regional biodiversity and ecological productivity illustrating the cyclic process of the sensitivity of environment to climate reflected in the ecological significance \cite{Klauco2013,Lemenkova2021c,Bumtsend}. The interconnectivity between climate, environmental and social factors described above illustrates the effects of the climate cycle on ecological system in Mongolia that demonstrate mutual feedbacks between the geographic and climate evolution and human aspects of life \cite{YU201977,XU2021104333,TUGJAMBA2021101360}.

Climate change studies currently experience an important challenge in methods of monitoring and mapping data using automated and advanced approaches of data analysis \cite{FENG2020102861,WANG201768,GUO2021141481}. Precise and accurate visualisation of geo-information on the geometric extent of the fields, types and degree of climate hazards derived from the open geospatial high-resolution datasets can help monitoring and improving climate change prognosis and environmental risk assessment both at national and regional scales including both Mongolia and the neighbouring region of Inner Mongolia, China \cite{JIE2021353,5631071,5964785}. 

\subsection{Objectives}
The primary objective of this study is climate and environmental mapping of Mongolia using scripting cartographic techniques by Generic Mapping Tools (GMT). Using advanced methods of GMT for mapping, this study presented maps of selected climate parameters covering the territory of Mongolia. The project aims to focus on the twofold aspects.

The first aspect is related to climate and topographic analysis aimed to detect variations in climate parameters for environmental monitoring at national scale (average minimal and maximal temperatures, wind speed, soil moisture, downward surface shortwave radiation (DSRAD), snow water equivalent (SWE), vapor pressure deficit, vapor pressure anomaly, monthly precipitation and Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI), which demonstrates the simplified soil water balance estimating relative soil moisture conditions. Thus, this paper explores spatial variability of the climatic variables in Mongolia for the period of 2019 using TerraClimate data.

The second aspect concerns technical issues of cartographic methods using GMT that affect methods of data processing and visualisation: GMT scripting syntax, selection of parameters for optimal cartographic representation of spatial phenomena and data modelling. Using effective and advanced cartographic data processing it is possible to better highlight distribution of geographic and environmental phenomena, such as geomorphology of the country with effects of terrain steepness and elevation by using correct color scheme, hydrologic network density and thematic variables, including soil moisture, climate setting.

This research presents a compilation of the climate datasets based on TerraClimate open source repository which records measurements of the meteorological and climate global data and stores them in NetCDF format. 
The methodology applied the compiled data observations over Mongolia and visualized experimental meteorological data using the Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) cartographic scripting toolset \cite{Wesseletal2019} to map a series of the eleven new maps (10 climate maps and a topographic map) using scripting techniques of GMT, applied in the existing cartographic studies \cite{Lemenkova2019a,Kasalica,Lemenkova2021d,Spinellis,Lemenkova2021e,Virden}. 

The study illustrated how climate variability in various ecological zones of Mongolia is strongly affected by the complex topography of the country varying notably in its different regions of the country: high-mountain areas \cite{UNKELBACH2021100073,UNKELBACH2021107076}, semi-arid mountainous steppe and shrublands \cite{Minderlein}, deserts and semi-deserts, steppes, grasslands \cite{WANG2022108783,LIU2021108567}, taiga forests \cite{GRADEL201724,DULAMSUREN2005376} and alpine regions \cite{KLINGE2022108113}. Specifically, variations in different climate parameters, described above were evaluated over the topographic landforms of Mongolia, by comparing presented maps showing distribution of the continuous fields of climate variables. The paper then discusses the actuality and significance of scripting cartographic methods for robust and rapid visualisation of the climate data, and explores the implications for serial mapping using large datasets and provides a feedback on the existing applications of GUI-based GIS \cite{FAN201696,TSUTSUMIDA2015196,Lemenkova2020c,Amarsaikhan,NAKAYAMA2021109404,Lemenkova2020d,PARK2019103630,OSULLIVAN2019101979}. 

To better understand driving factors affecting the environment of Mongolia, the study goal is to highlight regional variations in climate and environmental variables of this country well correlating with its complex and diversified topography. To do this, a series of maps has been created using GMT scripting techniques aimed to demonstrate a correlation between the topographic and climate factors that contribute to the environmental sustainability of Mongolia.

Compared to the scripting mapping, there have been many studies published on traditional mapping using GUI-based GIS mapping \cite{Tuvshin,Lemenkova2020e,Lemenkova2021f,HOLGUIN2018137}, which, however, do not replace scripting mapping in terms of automatization, speed of data processing and aesthetic quality of mapping. At the same time, the variability and amount of data in the world requires fast and reliable machine-based techniques of data processing, modeling and visualization. This sets up a constant need to actualize, update and increase the quantity and quality of the existing maps of Mongolia using high-resolution thematic data and advanced cartographic approaches. This task requires advanced, machine-based effective cartographic methods with minimized handmade routine for speedy and comprehensive thematic environmental mapping. 

Examples of the new methods of automatization in cartography can be illustrated by such open source scripting tools as GRASS GIS \cite{FRIGERI20111265,Lemenkova2020f}, GMT \cite{5369602,Lemenkova2021g,BECKER20051075,Lemenkova2021h,OKUBO200459}, R \cite{Tserenpurev,Lemenkova2019b} or by the plugins of QGIS \cite{FANG202031,Lemenkova2020i}. This article seeks to contribute to these attempts and continue methodological development of mapping by presenting a series of maps based on the TerraClimate datasets prepared using GMT scripting techniques. The TerraClimate data present regular updates of the global climate data in raster NetCDF format to the public. This study uses data from the TerraClimate processed by GMT for mapping Mongolia.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{General approach}
The methodology of this study is based on integration of the open source geospatial data obtained from the TerraClimate and GEBCO. High-resolution datasets form TerraClimate and GEBCO are crucial source of information in climate and environmental domain. Visualisation of such data enables to highlight changes in continuous fields from the analysis of correlation between the isolines and correspondence of extremal values. 

The choice of the cartographic tool GMT for data processing is explained by its scripting language that enables to process large volumes of data rapidly and effectively. The production of geospatial data has been increasing continuously in volume worldwide. This is mostly associated with an increase in velocity of data generation and variety in formats. The onset of big spatial data era results in challenges of processing these data which can only be used by semi-automated methods, such as GMT scripting enabling to rapidly exploit large amounts of spatial information. 

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_02.jpg}
	\caption{Average minimal temperature in Mongolia in 2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app2}. Source: author. \label{fig2}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

Scripting techniques of GMT are used to process high-precision spatial data for environmental, topographic and climate mapping of Mongolia. Data analysis is based on the process of extracting information by interpreting climate variables using features visualised by cartographic approaches, e.g. colour, geometric structure and patterns of changed variables as a continuous phenomena corresponding to the terrain relief and interpreting the retrieved information from the data as environmental analysis. For instance, scripting language of GMT enables effective data processing and visualisation of raster files in large spatial and temporal scales, which is an actual task for climate and environmental monitoring in real time regime, for instance by comparison data for multiple years. Algorithms of GMT-based scripting propose advanced ways of such task enabling spatial data processing, computer vision and automated plotting. This explains the choice of the data and selected software tools for this study.

\subsection{Technical workflow}
The topographic mapping (Fig. \ref{fig1}) was based on the General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans (GEBCO) grid \cite{Schenke2016}, which aggregates the SRTM data for the terrestrial areas at 15 arc-second (ca. 450 m) resolution. Other maps (Fig. \ref{fig2} to \ref{fig11}) were plotted using TerraClimate raster climate datasets in NetCDF format processed by GMT. 

The GMT module ‘grdcut’ was used to clip the region of Mongolia from the global grid using the following code: ‘gmt grdcut GEBCO\_2019.nc -R87.5/120/41.5/52.5 -Gmn\_relief.nc’ where the ‘-R’ flag controls the coordinates of the study area in WESN convention (87.5°–120°E, 41.5°–52.5°N). The application of module ‘psclip’ generates a selection of the study area using DCW contour of the country which was defined suing the code: ‘gmt psclip -R87.5/120/41.5/52.5 -JPoly/6.5i Mongolia.txt -O -K >> \$ps’. The river hydrological network was visualized using the ‘pscoast’ module: ‘gmt pscoast -R -J -Ia/thinner,blue -Na -N1/thicker,tomato -W0.1p -Df -O -K >> \$ps’.  	

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_03.jpg}
	\caption{Average max. temperature in Mongolia for 04.2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app3}. Source: author. \label{fig3}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

The isolines on the maps of temperature (Fig. \ref{fig2} and Fig. \ref{fig3}) were modeled using the ‘grdcontour’ module of GMT: 'gmt grdcontour mn\_tmin.nc -R -J -C2 -A2+f7p,0,black+gwhite@60 -Wthinnest,darkbrown -O -K >> \$ps'. Here the '-A' flag explains the annotations plotted for every 2nd line with ascertained transparency as reported in the existing works \cite{Lemenkova2020j}. 

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_04.jpg}
	\caption{Wind speed in Mongolia in 2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app4}. Source: author. \label{fig4}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

The texts were added by the 'pstext' module of GMT, e.g.: ‘gmt pstext -R -J -N -O -K -F+jTL+f9p,26,blue2+jLB -Gwhite@60 >> \$ps << EOF 92.3 50.1 Uvs Nuur EOF’ where the actually printed expression is defined between the coordinates in decimal system and the returned ‘EOF’ signal. The visualization was made using the 'makecpt' module: 'gmt makecpt -Cjet -T-43/-16/0.5 > pauline.cpt' and set up for 'jet' color table for the both figures (Fig. \ref{fig2} and \ref{fig3}) for comparability. Here the ‘-43/-16/0.5’ flag provides the range of data and the step of the interval in color visualization.   

Fig. \ref{fig4} showing the wind speed in m/s has been plotted using the wind speed data from the TerraClimate dataset: ‘gmt grdcut TerraClimate\_ws\_2018.nc -R87.5/120/41.5/52.5 -Gmn\_wind.nc’. The surrounding area has been set transparent using the ‘-t100’ flag to focus on the area of Mongolia. The cartographic ticks have been visualized using flag ‘-Bpxg10f5a5 -Bpyg10f2.5a2.5 -Bsxg5 -Bsyg5’ which signifies that major latitude and longitude lines are plotted every 10° degrees, text label annotations are plotted every 2.5° degrees and minor ticks are plotted every 5°. 

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_05.jpg}
	\caption{Soil moisture in Mongolia in 2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app5}. Source: author. \label{fig5}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

The soil moisture map of Mongolia in Fig. \ref{fig5} has been visualized using colormap 'sky-18' developed by Rafi at GraphicsFuel with the data extent 0/40. The conversion of the PostScript file to the JPG image file was made using GhostScript: ‘gmt psconvert MN\_soil.ps -A0.5c -E720 -Tj -Z’ where the ‘-E’ flag shows the output resolution of the file (here: 720 dpi) and the ‘-A’ flag gives the image margins (here: 0.5 cm) and ‘-T’ stands for the file format definition (here: JPG). 

Similarly, the downward surface shortwave radiation (DSR) has been visualized using GMT scripting for the year 2019 in Mongolia, as shown in Fig. \ref{fig6}. The DSR was mapped using specific dataset of the TerraClimate estimated of the total amount of shortwave radiation (both direct and diffuse) that reaches the Earth's surface (average values was 77 Wm-2) for the area of Mongolia. The model was visualized using colormap '22 hue sat value2' adopted from the color tables of IDL KST by Python with the data range from -40 Wm-2 to 113Wm-2. The information about the coordinate projection was added by the code: ‘-Lx14.0c/-2.0c+c10+w1000k+l"American polyconic projection. Scale (km)"+f’. The methods of estimation of the original DSR grids uses the remote sensing approach which is based on the estimating the signals received from the spectral channels in visible and Near Infra-Red (NIR), plus the data on the albedo and atmospheric composition used for calculation of the DSR at the terrain surface.  	

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_06.jpg}
	\caption{Downward surface shortwave radiation in Mongolia in 2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app6}. Source: author. \label{fig6}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

The Snow Water Equivalent (SWE) at end of month in Mongolia in 2019 (Fig. \ref{fig7}) was determined by visualizing the grid using ‘grdimage’ module: ’gmt grdimage mn\_swe.nc -Cpauline.cpt -R87.5/120/41.5/52.5 -JPoly/6.5i -I+a15+ne0.75 -Xc -P -O -K >> \$ps’ as described in existing papers \cite{Lemenkova2020l}, and adding isolines by the ‘grdcontour’ module: ‘gmt grdcontour mn\_swe.nc -R -J -C50 -A50+f7p,0,black+gwhite@60 -Wthinnest,darkbrown -O -K >> \$ps’. Furthermore,  the data range was also determined by the GDAL utility ‘gdalinfo’: ‘gdalinfo -stats mn\_swe.nc’, which shown the thresholds in data used for adjusting the Color Palette Table (cpt) identified as data extremities (here: up to 491). Methodologically, SWE depicts the estimated snowpack measurement over Mongolia showing the amount of water contained within the snowpack over the country.

The Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) in Mongolia (Fig. \ref{fig8}) was estimated using the difference between the calculated atmosphere moisture and the air capacity to contain the moisture if saturated. As such, it utilizes the information on the greenhouse air temperature, relative humidity and canopy air temperature. The VPD has correlations with crop transpiration and climate factors, such as warming, water shortage and drying of atmosphere \cite{Ghanem,Lietal2021,Song}. The techniques were applied using the explained GMT scripting. Vapor Pressure (VAP) shown in Fig. \ref{fig9} is represented using an advanced computation approach based on the equation computing VAP as the pressure from a vapor in thermodynamic equilibrium with solid or liquid phases. Since VAP is the atmospheric pressure exerted by water vapor, it is a method of measuring the humidity of the air and may indicate environmental setting in the country suitable or unsuitable for crop planting and agricultural activities. The visualization of the VAP map has been performed using colormap 'f-33-13-10' by the Gnuplot pm3d CPT which was developed as a scale by P. Mikulík (the data range here is from 0 to 4.6 for an RGB CPT). 

The precipitation map (Fig. \ref{fig10}) accounts for a monthly data from the TerraClimate as of 2019 tabulated in NetCDF format and plotted using similar GMT techniques. The grid annotations were defined using flag 'FORMAT\_GEO\_MAP=ddd:mm:ssF' controlling general map formatting in ‘psbasemap’ module of GMT. The Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI) visualized in Fig. \ref{fig11} is a function of temperature and precipitation. the PDSI is nowadays a widely accepted in climatology index originally developed by the American meteorologist Wayne Palmer \cite{Palmer1965}. The PDSI normally differs from -10 (very dry) to +10 (extreme wet) however, the actual data may vary regionally. Thus, for the case of Mongilia in 2019, the PDSI data ranged from minimum=-8.100 to maximum=5.800, with mean=-0.626 and SD=2.041, according to the data inspection by GDAL (gdlainfo).

\section{Results}

Climate input datasets from the TerraClimate have been visualized using methodology described in the previous section. All the maps were plotted with identical cartographic setting using American Polyconic projection for comparability reasons. This implies, for example, that the observed variations of the temperatures, soils moisture, PDSI, and wind force and climate water deficit showing soils quality were compared within the area of Mongolia suitable for overlay. The visualized grids were compared to observe specific ranges of the climate variables by analyzing the respective values by the relevant maps within Mongolia, as explained below.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_07.jpg}
	\caption{Snow water equivalent in Mongolia in 2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app7}. Source: author. \label{fig7}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

The topographic map of Mongolia (sourced from \cite{GEBCO}) was visualized for the clipped region of the country using ‘wiki-schwarzwald-d050 cpt’. The GEBCO data are widely used in the geosciences due to the unprecedentedly high resolution, reliability and precision of the raster \cite{Liuetal2019,Nock,Lemenkova2020m,Saito}. The inspected data range shows the highest point at Khüiten Peak (4,374 m) in the SW of the country on the border with China in the Altai Range, the topographic mean is 1319.129 m, SD is 571.535 m, according to the GDAL data inspection. 
The minimal temperatures in Mongolia for the April 2019 (Fig. \ref{fig2}) were identified as minimum=-43.000°C, maximum=-16.100°C, mean=-27.088°C, StdDev=4.858°C based on the TerraClimate dataset. The coldest region is noted as surroundings of the Uus Nuur and Tes River in the NW of the country. 

The maximal temperatures for the April 2019 (Fig. \ref{fig3}) were shown the following results for the April 2019: minimum=-32.100°C, maximum=1.000°C, mean=-15.443°C, StdDev=4.977 °C. The data range for the wind speed (Fig. \ref{fig4}) shown following results: minimum=0.000 m/s, maximum=4.600m/s, Mean=2.019m/s, StdDev=1.031 m/s. The wind demonstrates the highest values in the Gobi Desert region in the SE and the eastern region of the country (Choibalsan region) which well corresponds with the leveled topography of these regions. In contrast, the lowest values of the wind speed are notable in the NW of the country around the cities Khovd, \"Olgii and Ulaangom, as well as in the Altai Mts region.

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
	\includegraphics[width=14.0 cm]{Fig_08.jpg}
	\caption{Vapor pressure deficit in Mongolia in 2019. Mapping: GMT by script available in Appendix \ref{app8}. Source: author. \label{fig8}}
\end{figure}   
\unskip

The soil moisture in Mongolia (Fig. \ref{fig5})  volumetric water content in the soils of Mongolia with the following data range: from 0 to the maximum values at 155.000 cm3/cm3, with mean at 6.393 cm3/cm3 and SD at 13.850 cm3/cm3. The highest values are notable in the northern regions of the country around the cities Mörön, Erdenet, Ulaanbaatar as well as in central regions of the Bayankhongor and Arvaikheer. The notably high values of the soil are clearly correspond to the river valleys and lakes (Selenge, Khövsgöl, Kherlen, Onon) while the lowest values close to zero are visible in the southern region of the Gobi Desert. 

The inspection of the results on the downward surface shortwave radiation (DSRAD) in Mongolia in 2019 (Fig. \ref{fig6}) shows the following outputs in the statistical data range: Minimum=40.000 Wm-2, Maximum=113.000 Wm-2, Mean=77.007 Wm-2, StdDev=15.877 Wm-2. The general trend in the data distribution demonstrates a clearly NS direction with highest values corresponding the southern regions (Gobi Desert and surroundings) while the lowest values are in the north of the country (Khövsgöl, Selenga River valley and its tributaries, Onon River, Uvs Nuur).

The Snow Water Equivalent (SWE) shown in Fig. \ref{fig7}. demonstrated the statistical analysis as follows: data varies from the minimum=0 to the maximum=491.000 mm, Mean=16.552 mm, SD=21.985 mm. \section{Discussion}
The study presented a data-driven mapping of climate and environmental setting of Mongolia. Cartographic technical approach of this study is based on scripting processing supported by GMT. To this aim, the study applied existing datasets available from GEBCO and TerraClimate for climate and environmental monitoring  using data for 2019. In this way the presented maps contributed to the general goals on sustainable development, environmental and biodiversity protection and climate mitigation in Mongolia. 

At present, scripting applications of cartography in climate and environmental mapping of central Asian countries with sensitive climate conditions are still being developed. This research contributed to the increase of such applications by presenting a study based on the integration of environmental and climate geospatial data obtained from the open sources and climate monitoring surveys and automated cartographic data analysis actively using scripting algorithms of GMT for high-precision mapping. 

This research presented a series of maps linking practices of climate monitoring with cartographic techniques for knowledge-based environmental monitoring of Mongolia. The outcomes, presented as 11 new maps, aimed to support the implementation of precise climate and environmental monitoring of Mongolia. In such a way it contributed to the development of actual non-destructive environmental monitoring systems based on cartographic analysis in previous studies of Mongolia and neighbouring regions \cite{MENG2021107908,DASHPUREV2021108331,SAINNEMEKH2022104654,Batunacun}. Actual and regular updates of mapping helps to increase the control on possible drought distribution and climate characteristics, such as temperature, vapour, snow water equivalent, etc. It may contribute to the agricultural monitoring system in Mongolia or Inner Mongolia (China), following the existing studies \cite{Rina,Long,Zheng}. In such a way, the extended study can reach the goal to increase harvest production levels and quality using geospatial data processed by GMT.

Specifically, this study contributed to the smart climate monitoring system of Mongolia in following aspects: 
Discrimination of variations of climate variables using several maps visualising data in 2019 by GMT which enables to detect and separate regions with lower and higher temperatures, risks, of droughts by visualising data on DSRAD, VAP and VPD, soil water balance estimating relative soil moisture in the Gobi Desert and other arid regions of the country compared to the plains and mountainous regions. Specific tasks in cartographic workflow using GMT scripting include accurate techniques of map plotting and visualising various elements on the map by lines of the code. Computer-based semi-automated data processing in cartography using algorithms of GMT scripting significantly facilitates and improves technical workflow of such tasks compared to the traditional GUI-based GIS which still remains a tedious handmade routine. 

Besides, the advantage of using open geospatial data, such as TerraClimate and GEBCO, for climate and environmental monitoring consist in effectiveness of data analysis: visualising open datasets creates methods of low-cost and contactless approach to regions in remote locations and enables their monitoring and mapping. Using open source data processed by automated methods of cartographic scripting may further improve developing a system of detecting droughts and monitoring environmental setting visualised as a series of thematic maps for preventing climate and environmental risks through accurate data visualising and prognosis. In such a way, environmental and climate monitoring reduces the need for fieldwork in remotely located and data-scarce areas with restricted access, such as Mongolia Gobi Desert \cite{HEINER202078}.

Moreover, topographic and climate mapping using scripting GMT methods and open geospatial data for environmental analysis and monitoring fits to the increase of digitalisation and visualisation of data for sustainable agriculture, since it strongly depends on regional climate and topographic setting of the country. The strategy of regular cartographic mapping of the country aims at increase of the knowledge which consists in smart environmental and climate monitoring system based on the available datasets for sustainable ecosystems, identification of climate hazards, analysis of biodiversity and vegetation mapping in Mongolia. Integration of various datasets into the inter-disciplinary project on environmental and climate monitoring of Mongolia ensures the dissemination of results of climate change in various regions of the country.

As a recommendation for future studies would be analysis of multi-temporal changes in climate and environmental setting of the country using datasets taken for various year periods. Spatio-temporal multi-source data analysis supports comprehensive environmental and climate analysis of Mongolia in real-time regime, which can be applied for agriculture and crop management support systems. Besides, overlapping historical archive climate and environmental data with actual data using computer vision techniques and machine learning algorithms, can enable to perform a retrospective times-series analysis for predictive mapping and agricultural prognosis of Mongolia. Thus, the semi-automated cartographic data processing by GMT for environmental and climate mapping, as demonstrated in this paper, can be furthermore applied in a real-time regime using time series analysis of climate data covering several years.  

Furthermore, the presented map series may contribute to the data analysis in agricultural sector of Mongolia and international community of Central Asian studies. Thematic maps visualising variables in the identical projection and spatial extent enable demonstrate geospatial data from the multi-disciplinary sources, mostly of topographic and climate origin. Such datasets contribute to the development of data-based solutions for agricultural monitoring of Mongolia through visualisation of regional climate and topographic setting of the country and support of adaptive crop management. Acquiring new knowledge in the fields of sustainable development and environmental monitoring of Mongolia is largely based on creating a series of maps using algorithms of the advanced cartographic workflow.

\section{Conclusions}

To address the problem of modern cartographic data processing and visualisation in physical geography, environmental and climate studies, this study demonstrated the use of GMT scripting toolset, selected due to its advanced methods of cartographic data processing, and open source data, such as TerraClimate and GEBCO, processed for Earth monitoring in Mongolia. 

The GMT based scripting approach of climate mapping presented in this study with a case of Mongolia provides both a qualitative and a quantitative basis for visualising areas of notable climate variations over the country to target for high-resolution environmental and climate mapping of Mongolia. The proposed methodology based on the TerraClimate dataset can be used for other periods since 1958 up to now aimed both at retrospective mapping and for estimating mapping proposing prognosis of future scenarios in climate change. The presented application of machine learning in cartography specifically was used to process several large global raster datasets of TerraClimate with the threefold aims:     

\begin{itemize}
	\item	to estimate the utilization of the machine learning cartography for big data processing;
	\item	to perform comparative spatial analysis of the environmental factors in Mongolia;
	\item	to present regional climate variability by focusing mapping on the regions where the extremes in climate variables are the most notable due to the contrasting topography: major mountain ranges of the Altai, Khangai and Khentii, Central Mongolia, northern riverine network (Selenga River with tributaries) as well as the Gobi Desert region.
\end{itemize}

Hence, the processed and visualized datasets illustrated the impact of the local topography in Mongolia on such variables as wind speed and radiation, average minimal and maximal temperatures, wind speed, soil moisture dynamics, distribution of the downward surface shortwave radiation, snow water equivalent, vapor pressure deficit and anomaly, monthly precipitation and Palmer Drought Severity Index (PDSI). 

The comparative analysis for climate data based on the presented eleven new maps plotted in identical cartographic American polyconic projection provided a cartographic approach for the visualization of the spatio-temporal climatic and environmental data collected for Mongolia. Climate variables mapped within the special areas of Mongolia (Gobi, Altai, Khentii, Khangai, basins of major rivers, such as Selenga, Onon, Kherlen, and Tes, the region of Central Mongolia within the Ulaanbaatar district, eastern region of Choibalsan) revealed a complex variation of the continuous fields in climate variables which well corresponds to the topographic and hydrologic specific setting of the country. 

Future applications of the presented study consist in the presented detailed and updated maps that can be used for further analysis, understanding and discussing of the character and reasons of climate and environmental variations in Mongolia, as well as their correlations with the contrasting topography of the country. Furthermore, the plotted series of the 11 maps made using advanced cartographic solutions of GMT will assist in continue of the developing existing regional environmental models and studies on Mongolia \cite{MAS,Pfeiffer}. Furthermore, the study aims to broaden our understanding of environmental and climate spatial diversity in various regions of Mongolia based on the existing studies \cite{Altanbold,Jamyansuren,Jugder}. 

Climate dynamics visualized in eleven thematic maps on 2019 are presented based on the scripting algorithms of GMT modules by consecutive iteration of the lined of code explained in the methodology section for technical reference and for future applications in similar cartographic works. The machine-based data processing applied in various geoscience application \cite{Lemenkova2019c,Lemenkov2021} demonstrate effectiveness and automation of data analysis, modelling and visualization. In this study machine learning as cartographic scripting approaches of GMT was applied to reduce the workload of the visualization through the increased automation in cartographic workflow approaches presented in this manuscript with a case study of Mongolia.  	

This paper determined the geospatial variability of climate and geomorphologic factors that influence regional environmental differences for Mongolia. The results distinguished variations in climate sets caused by the distribution of the landmasses in mountain ranges of Altai, Khangai and Khentii, distinct from the regions of northern Mongolia with dense hydrologic network, central Mongolia around Ulaanbaatar, eastern region, steppes, semi-deserts and Gobi Desert which approves the existing studies on the environmental and climate topics \cite{Cook,Ulaankhuu}. 

A machine learning approach of coding applied for cartographic tasks was tested by GMT used for raster TerraClimate dataset processing and by detailed geographical analysis of Mongolia. Spatial environmental and climate analysis, which determines relative distribution of PDSI and temperature extremes, precipitation and soil moisture, wind speed and DSRAD, SWE, VAP and VPD compared with landmass parameters represent powerful cartographic tools to address complex regional climate and environmental issues in Mongolia, a country with contrasting topography, extreme climate conditions and unique environmental setting.
